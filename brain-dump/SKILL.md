# Brain Dump

When I ask you to take a brain dump, help me move what is in my head into my
knowledge base as clean, structured entries. I will give you raw material: a
dictated walkthrough, a voice memo or call transcript, rough notes, and often
the real files I use for this work. Read it, pull out the actual knowledge, and
write it up so it lands in my knowledge base looking like everything else. Ask
me anything you need before you start, and do not assume.

## What to do

1. **Take the raw material.** Work from what I give you: a transcript, pasted
   notes, and any files I attach. If I mention files I did not include, ask for
   them before you start.
2. **Assume you are working on copies.** Before I bring material to you, I make
   copies, because it is easy to move forward with AI and hard to go back. Still,
   treat everything I give you as read-only: do not change my source files, and
   preserve the version before any edit so I can always return to it.
3. **Pull out the real knowledge, not a transcript summary.** Capture the
   process, the steps, the judgment calls, the exceptions, and the "why" that I
   only know from doing the work.
4. **Write it up against my knowledge base's shape, and keep every raw source.**
   Give the entry the same shape as the rest of my knowledge base: a title, a
   short purpose, the process, key decisions, gotchas, and links to the files it
   relates to. Then keep all the raw material I gave you, the recording or voice
   memo, the transcript, and the files, filed alongside in a structured place
   (wherever those sources already live), so any claim can be traced back.
5. **Fold it in where it fits, and create what is new.** A single source usually
   touches more than one place.
   - Where the material belongs in a document I already have, fold it into that
     existing entry in the right spot.
   - Where the material is genuinely new, create a new entry, and a new area of
     the knowledge base if it needs one, and propose where it should live.
   - Update the index for anything you add or move.
6. **Reconcile what this makes outdated.** Scan the related parts of my knowledge
   base for anything this recording or document now changes, and update the
   outdated information, noting what changed ("Previously X. As of [date], Y.")
   and keeping the earlier version, so the knowledge base stays true and shows
   how things used to work.
7. **Interview me to fill the gaps.** A brain dump is always missing pieces. Ask
   me the few questions you need to make the entry true and complete, rather than
   guessing.
8. **Propose, do not file silently.** Show me the draft, where each part will go,
   and what you plan to update, and file it only when I say go.

## Rules

- Assume copies: do not modify my original files, and preserve the prior version
  before changing anything.
- Use my words and my facts. Do not invent steps, names, or numbers that are not
  in the material or my answers.
- Keep every raw source. File the recording, transcript, and notes with the entry
  so it can be traced.
- Ask before you act. Ask before you file, before you overwrite an existing
  entry, and before you update anything my new material makes outdated. When you
  are not sure, ask rather than assume.
- Do not send, post, or share anything outside my own files.

## Quality check before you finish

- The entry follows the same shape as the rest of my knowledge base.
- Every claim traces to my material or my answers, not to a guess.
- New material was folded into the right existing entries, and genuinely new
  material got its own entry (and area, if needed).
- Anything this source made outdated was updated, the change recorded, and the
  old version preserved.
- Every raw source (recording, transcript, files) is filed alongside and traceable.
- The index points to what you added or moved, and I approved where it lives.
- The gaps you could not fill are listed as open questions, not quietly filled in.

If the raw material is too thin to make a real entry, tell me what is missing and
ask me the questions that would fill it.
